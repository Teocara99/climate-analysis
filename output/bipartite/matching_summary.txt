INTERPRETATION
============================================================

Paragraph 1 — Technology Space:
  The SPECTER2 embeddings reveal 12 distinct technology communities in climate tech VC, spanning clean energy hardware (solar, battery, hydrogen), software-driven solutions (grid management, carbon accounting, AgriTech), and frontier-science ventures (fusion, novel materials, DAC). Clusters differ markedly in failure rates and capital requirements, suggesting that technology type is a first-order predictor of outcomes.

Paragraph 2 — Matching Patterns:
  The bipartite network shows that most investor communities concentrate flows into 2-3 technology clusters rather than diversifying broadly. Geographic co-location (same-region investor-startup pairs) correlates with lower failure rates, consistent with information-advantage theories. Green VC communities disproportionately fund frontier-tech clusters (high novelty), while traditional IVC communities anchor in later-stage, revenue-generating software clusters.

Paragraph 3 — Complementarity vs Alignment:
  The regression results reveal a nuanced picture: geographic alignment (same-region matching) reduces failure, but novelty complementarity (investors with different novelty preferences funding high-novelty startups) correlates with higher exit rates. This suggests that the optimal match is neither pure alignment nor pure complementarity, but rather geographic proximity combined with experiential diversity — investors who understand frontier science but operate in the startup's local ecosystem generate the best risk-adjusted outcomes.
